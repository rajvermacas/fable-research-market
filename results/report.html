What it did

The machine

Rules, in full

Universe: every currently listed NSE main-board (EQ) stock, ~2,060 names — no index membership. All signals use closes up to day T; every trade executes at the close of T+1. No leverage, no derivatives, no shorting.

Selection — monthly, at month-end close

  1. Eligible: traded today · ≥ 1 year of history · top-300 by 63-day median rupee turnover (point-in-time) · price > ₹10 · above own 200-day SMA.
  2. Score: mean of 3, 6 and 12-month returns, each skipping the most recent month, divided by 6-month realized volatility. Must be positive.
  3. Portfolio: top-15 equal weight, with a rank buffer — holdings stay while still in the top 30, halving turnover.

Risk stack — exposure = product of four scalars, daily

  1. Trend gate (0/1): Sensex above its 200-day SMA (1% hysteresis) and not 10% below its 63-day high — a self-healing fast-crash breaker.
  2. Breadth (0→1): share of the liquid pool above their own 200-day SMA, mapped 25% → 0, 55% → 1. Sizes the book by market internals.
  3. Vol target: min(1, 25% / EWMA vol of the book). Cuts within days when volatility explodes.
  4. Equity brake: half size while the strategy's own curve is under its 200-day average — the only signal that sees a momentum-factor winter.
  • Per stock: 25% trailing stop off the post-entry peak; stale listings force-exited at −10%. Idle cash earns 6% (T-bill proxy).
Why each layer exists

Every layer maps to a failure it fixes

The naive version of this strategy — same selection, classic Sensex-200DMA filter — returns 28% but with a −45% drawdown. Each layer was added to fix a diagnosed structural failure, not to tune a number. Removing any one of them re-opens its wound (all else at base):

Plateau, not a point

Robustness evidence

One-at-a-time — 65 configs

  • Every parameter swept across wide ranges: N 10–30, lookbacks 3–12 months, skip 0–42d, pool 150–800, SMAs 100–300, vol targets, stops, gates on/off, costs 0.15–0.50%/side.
  • 55 of 65 land at CAGR > 27% with DD < 28%; 28 pass the strict mandate outright.
  • Minimum in-sample Sharpe across all 65: 1.37.
  • The failures are informative, not random: pools ≤ 200 names, or a risk layer switched off.

Random joint draws — 200 configs

  • Uniform samples over the entire hypercube, deliberately including crippled corners with risk layers disabled.
  • Minimum in-sample Sharpe of all 200: 1.06 — the alpha is structural, not parameter luck.
  • With all layers armed, 91% keep the forward drawdown under 25%.
  • Rebalancing 10 sessions after month-end: 31.4% / -22.8% — no date luck.
Forward test, honestly

What held and what didn't

Held: the drawdown mandate (−18.0% vs 25% allowed), the Sharpe (0.89), and the edge over the market (+7.3pp/yr vs Sensex). Missed: the absolute 30% bar. Of 200 random configs, only 10 exceeded 30% forward — every one of them lives in the deep small-cap pool (500–800 names), and all but one carry in-sample drawdowns that would have disqualified them in 2021. Widening this strategy's pool from 300 to 800 names (one parameter) gives 33.7% / −22.4% in-sample and 27.8% / −18.4% forward — a documented upside configuration, with eyes open: that end of the market is precisely where survivorship bias, slippage and capacity constraints bite hardest. The honest conclusion is that 30%/yr is a property of the 2003–2021 mid/small-cap momentum regime, not a harvestable constant; the strategy's durable properties are the contained drawdown and the persistent market-relative edge.

Read before believing

Limitations, in order of importance

1

Survivorship bias

Yahoo serves only currently listed stocks; companies delisted between 2000 and 2026 are invisible to the backtest. Mitigations: the universe is all ~2,060 listed names (today's losers included), eligibility is point-in-time, a trailing stop caps blowup paths, and the forward window is nearly bias-free. But the momentum premium here lives in mid/small caps — where delistings concentrate — and a large-cap-only variant earns 19.2%, not 31%. Treat the in-sample CAGR as an upper estimate.

2

Data quality, repaired but not perfect

Yahoo's NSE history contains unadjusted splits and bad prints — hundreds of fake ±50%+ days concentrated in 2003–2007. A conservative repair layer fixes spike-reversals and exact-fraction cliffs, caps residual one-day gains at +40%, and never caps losses; genuine crashes (Yes Bank −56%) are preserved. 640 repairs, all logged.

3

Thin early coverage

Yahoo's NSE data reaches only ~60–90 symbols before 2002, so 2000–2002 is effectively a large-cap backtest; coverage grows to ~480 by 2003 and 900+ by 2010.

4

Assumptions that flatter

Idle cash at 6%/yr (Indian T-bill average) adds ~4pp vs assuming 0% (27.2% without it). Costs at 0.30%/side are fair for the modern era, optimistic for 2000–2005. Monthly rotation of mid/small caps is personal-capital scale, not fund scale.

5

A live, unfinished year

2026 numbers run to 2026-08-28 on as-is Yahoo data. The strategy is currently ~half exposed with its equity curve below its own 200-day average — the machinery says "momentum winter", and the honest chart shows it.
